Qualifying a replacement ingredient

Two hours. Works in a browser with the demo dataset; no bench work needed. Your instructor will tell you where to sign in. Print this page or keep it open beside the app.

The situation

A supplier has discontinued the plasticizer in a coating. Two candidate substitutes exist. The replacement has to meet the original specifications: adhesion at least 8.0, viscosity between 2000 and 2600 cP, dry time at most 40 minutes, gloss at least 85. You have 72 historical lots; 61 completed and 11 failed before they could be measured. Your job is to get from that history to a candidate you would be willing to sign your name under.

Part 1 · Read the history (15 min)

  1. Sign in, then New project → Start with demo → Create v0.6 demo project.
  2. Open the same history as a spreadsheet: demo_coatings_reformulation.csv (Download raw file, then open in Excel or Sheets). Sort by status.
  3. On Project overview, read the six numbers at the top and the qualification gates table. Nothing is qualified yet; that is the starting line.

Q1. The eleven failed lots carry a failure_reason but no measurements. List the three reasons. Then look at mix_temperature_c and supplier_family for the failed lots: what pattern do you see, and what would you tell the lab to avoid in the next batch?

Q2. The caption says what the probability is and is not. In one sentence: what would it be a mistake to conclude from a candidate showing 0.62?

  1. After the last result, open Calibration. The models retrained on your results.

Q3. Look at the run-level calibration. Were the model's predictions for your entered results too optimistic, too pessimistic, or about right? What would you want to see before trusting the next batch's probabilities?

Part 4 · Judge the replicates (25 min)

  1. On Experiment loop, choose your best candidate. Open Create missing linked replicates, set the target to 3, and create them.
  2. Enter results for all three, and for each one set Qualification stage to confirmation before saving (the confirmation gate only counts replicate groups at its own stage). Keep two of them close together and make the third one clearly off on one response (for example viscosity 2250, 2280, then 3400).
  3. Go to Qualification and open Running records — look before computing. Then read the confirmation gate's remaining requirements.

Q4. What did the gate say about your odd replicate, and in what order does it judge things (running record, limits from the other replicates, CV)? Why would a CV computed across all three have hidden the problem or exaggerated it?

  1. Fix the odd replicate to a consistent value and watch the gate's status change.

Part 5 · Sign and export (20 min)

  1. Approvals & dossier. Note the evidence hash shown for the project.
  2. Sign the discovery stage: choose the signature meaning, type your account name, re-enter your password, Sign approval.
  3. Generate dossier package and open the ZIP. Find SHA256SUMS.txt and the approvals table.
  4. Go back to Experiment loop and change one result. Return to Approvals & dossier.

Q5. What happened to the evidence hash and to your signature's matches_current_evidence flag after you changed a result? Why is a visible warning that the newest evidence has not been signed more useful to an auditor than a signature that silently still looks valid?

Hand in

  • This worksheet (Q1 to Q5 answered).
  • Your dossier ZIP or .eln archive. Your instructor will check that the evidence hash inside matches the signed approval.
  • One paragraph: which candidate would you take to the confirmation stage, and what would you still need before recommending it to production?

Everything in the demo is synthetic data built for teaching. The tool is decision support; it does not replace chemical-safety review, regulatory review or a validated quality system.
