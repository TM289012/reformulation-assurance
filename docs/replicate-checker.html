How this works (and what it can't tell you)

The check compares the difference between the two group means against the noise implied by your own replicates: the standard error of the difference, computed from each group's spread and count (the same quantity a Welch t-test uses). The verdict uses a deliberately conservative bar for small samples: a difference more than ~3 standard errors is called likely real, 2–3 is called suggestive, under 2 is called indistinguishable from noise.

It also answers the more useful planning questions: the smallest difference your current noise and replicate count could reliably show, and roughly how many replicates per formula you would need to settle the difference you actually observed.

Limits: this is a screen, not a proof. It assumes independent runs measured the same way, and spread estimated from 3–4 replicates is rough. It says nothing about whether the difference matters practically — that's your spec's job. And if your measurements were taken over days or weeks, check your baseline for drift first with the Baseline Drift Checker, because drift masquerades as a formula difference.
