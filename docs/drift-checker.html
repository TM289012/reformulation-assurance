Baseline Drift Checker

Paste repeated measurements of something that should be stable — a control batch, a reference standard, an instrument baseline. Find out in seconds whether it's drifting or it's just noise.

One per line, or separated by commas or spaces. 8+ points give reliable limits; 15+ is better.

How this works (and what it can't tell you)

This is an XmR individuals chart, the classic process-behavior chart (Shewhart; see Donald Wheeler's books). The limits are mean ± 2.66 × average moving range, computed from your own data. They describe how much routine, point-to-point noise your measurements already contain.

Two signals are checked: any point outside the limits (something changed abruptly), and 8 or more consecutive points on the same side of the mean (something is slowly drifting). If neither fires, the honest reading is that your variation looks routine and chasing individual ups and downs will just chase noise.

Limits: it assumes your values are in time order and describe one thing that should be stable. It won't detect drift smaller than your noise floor with few points, and it doesn't model autocorrelation. Small samples have hard floors (per Wheeler): the first or last point cannot flag before 6 values, a middle point cannot flag before 8, and the limits only stabilize around 17 or more — so below those counts this page says so instead of pretending. A verdict here is a reason to look, not a root cause.
