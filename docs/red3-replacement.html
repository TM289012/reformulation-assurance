Reformulation Assurance

Replacing FD&C Red No. 3

A 15-minute walkthrough of a color replacement in the free sandbox: the trial history, the replicate checks, and a sign-off that still says why the winner won when a buyer or an inspector asks a year from now.

Open the Red No. 3 example in the sandbox

The sandbox is shared with other visitors and resets periodically. Never enter real formulas in it. For real work, run the app on your own machine; the quickstart takes about ten minutes.

The deadline, in one paragraph

On January 15, 2025 the FDA revoked the authorization for FD&C Red No. 3 in food and ingested drugs. Food manufacturers have until January 15, 2027 to reformulate, and makers of ingested drugs until January 18, 2028 (FDA constituent update). State rules on other synthetic dyes follow from 2027 onward. For a small brand that means a color match done under time pressure, usually with natural colorants that behave differently from the dye they replace, and a record that has to survive retailer questionnaires and inspections long after the people who ran the trials have moved on.

Why the record matters as much as the color

Matching the shade is the easy half. The hard half shows up months later: which lots were tried, why each one was run, which result was the one the team actually trusted, who signed off and what they meant by it, and what changed since. In most small teams those answers live in a spreadsheet and three people's memories. The tool keeps them in one place as the work happens, and binds every signature to a hash of the exact evidence on the table at that moment, so nothing can be quietly edited afterwards.

The example project

A strawberry gummy colored with Red No. 3. The team has been screening two natural colorants, a black carrot anthocyanin extract and a beet juice concentrate, across cook temperatures and hold times. The history has 90 lots: 18 legacy lots that still contain Red No. 3 and define the color standard, and 72 trials, 8 of which failed outright (color lost during a hot cook, an earthy off-note, a deposit that would not set). Failed lots are evidence too, so they are in the file.

SpecificationLimitWhy
delta_e_vs_standardat most 3.0Color difference from the legacy standard, the usual commercial tolerance for a drop-in match.
a_starat least 38Redness. A match that is close in distance but weak in red reads pink on the shelf.
color_retention_pctat least 85Color kept after four weeks at 40 °C in a light cabinet. Anthocyanins and betanin fade differently, and this is where beet-heavy trials fall down.
flavor_scoreat least 7.5Trained-panel score, so an earthy beet note cannot be traded for color.

Every number in the dataset is synthetic. The script that generates it, make_demo_food_color.py, spells out the simple model behind it (anthocyanins redder at low pH and tougher in the cook, betanin bluish and heat-sensitive, Red No. 3 strong but light-sensitive), so you can see exactly what you are looking at and swap in your own history when you are ready.

Walkthrough

  1. Enter the sandbox. Open the example and click Enter the demo. The food project is selected; the cosmetics one is in the sidebar if you want it later.
  2. Read the history on Project overview, in the Experiments on record table. Sort by status, then read the notes on the failed lots and notice what the "color lost during cook" lots have in common: a lot of beet and a cook above about 120 °C.
  3. Recommendations, then Generate next batch. Model fitting takes 30 to 60 seconds. You get five candidates with different purposes (balanced, highest success probability, lowest disruption, highest information value, lowest cost), each with predictions, uncertainty and a modeled probability of meeting every specification. Read the caption under the table: the probability assumes the responses are independent, and the tool says so. Then Approve and freeze batch, which locks the predictions so they can be scored against reality later.
  4. Experiment loop. Pick a candidate, set its status to completed and enter plausible results, for example delta_e_vs_standard 2.1, a_star 44, color_retention_pct 90, flavor_score 8.0, then Save experiment update. The models retrain and the Calibration page gains a real prospective point.
  5. Replicates. On the same page, set a target of three replicates for your best candidate and Create missing replicates. Enter three readings that agree to the last digit (a* 44.0, 44.0, 44.0): the gate reports chunky data instead of accepting a coefficient of variation of zero. Then make one replicate wild (color difference 2.1, 2.2, 4.9): the Qualification page names the odd replicate and will not pass the stage.
  6. Approvals & dossier. Sign approval for the discovery stage: it asks for your typed name, your password and what the signature means, and binds all of it to the SHA-256 of the evidence as it stands. Then Generate dossier package and open the zip: every experiment, prediction, replicate verdict, approval and the audit trail, with checksums. Generate .eln archive gives the same evidence as one entry for eLabFTW or another lab notebook.
  7. Change one result after signing. Go back to the Experiment loop, edit a measurement, and return to the approvals page. The signature now shows as stale against the current evidence. That is the whole point: the record says what was true when someone put their name to it.

Running it on your own Red No. 3 project

Install locally (nothing leaves your machine; the data is a SQLite file you own) and use New project, Import your CSV. One row per lot, with the ingredient percentages (they should sum to a fixed total), the process conditions, the measured responses, and a status column so failed lots count as evidence. For a color replacement the responses are usually the color difference against your retained standard on your own colorimeter, a* or another chroma measure, retention after your storage protocol, and a sensory score. The import wizard infers bounds from your history and lets you set the specifications, and from there the loop is the one above.
